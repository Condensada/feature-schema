SCHEMA Digest handbook

Editorial guide for selecting, analyzing, and submitting Digest articles.

Purpose

SCHEMA Digest is the publication's art criticism and journalism corner. It offers critical reviews and analysis of literature such as novels, fiction, self-help books, films, and television or streaming series. A Digest should turn a work into an accessible, in-depth overview: structured, sharp, detailed, and compact. It should develop writers' critical thinking and connect meaningfully with the SAMCIS community.

What makes a strong Digest

  • Technical strength: Organize the article clearly and write it well.
  • Symbolic depth: Explore meanings beyond the most obvious reading.
  • Original insight: Offer a thoughtful interpretation that invites curiosity rather than repeating familiar opinions.
  • Balanced evaluation: Support your conclusions with specific observations while making your own perspective and overall impression clear.

Summarize without spoiling

Write for readers who may not have read or watched the source. Briefly introduce the premise, pivotal characters, relationships, and a few important turning points, but do not reveal the entire story. Choose details carefully to build interest and leave the central discoveries intact.

Analyze the work

Identify what the source is trying to express and examine relevant dimensions such as social, economic, cultural, psychological, emotional, spiritual, behavioral, or philosophical themes. Explain whether the work achieves its main idea, support your evaluation with specific details, and include your own considered response.

Style and use of source material

  • Use clear, accessible prose. Distinctive vocabulary is welcome in moderation; do not make readers work through dense wording.
  • Use a clever, fitting title and subheadings to guide the reader and add voice.
  • Keep quotations and other borrowed material limited. Cite memorable lines accurately, and make sure the article's analysis and original writing remain its focus.

Choosing a source

A source may be a novel, fiction story, self-help book, motion picture, or television/streaming series. Choose work that:

  • Has a clear structure and enough substance for technical and symbolic analysis.
  • Connects to the assigned theme.
  • Offers distinctive or innovative ideas and room for an original interpretation.
  • Engages with relevant social or political questions and supports thoughtful discussion.

Review platforms can help with background research and gauging reception. The source material itself should remain the basis of the article's analysis.

Pitch and approval process

  1. Develop a list of ten possible sources and assess each against the selection criteria.
  2. Choose your top three and tell the section editor why you recommend them.
  3. The section editor reviews the choices and may challenge whether a source is suitable.
  4. When available, seek Editorial Board Admin approval; otherwise, the section editor makes the final decision.
  5. Discuss and confirm the selected source with the section editor before beginning the article.

Submission and editing

  • Submit the finished article one to two days before its planned posting date to allow time for editorial review and revisions.
  • Upload the article to the designated SCHEMA Google Drive submission folder.
  • Notify the section editor after uploading your work.
  • Follow the editorial workflow and assignment dates in the Planner.
